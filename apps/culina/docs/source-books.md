# Books as cited authorities for Culina's skills

Research of 2026-11-19 for the owner's request: find books that work well _as cited authorities_ for skills (not as recipe collections). Nothing in the repository was changed.

## Decided by the owner on 2026-11-19

These are the owner's decisions after reading the research below. They are the only part of this file that is not the research itself.

- **Buying order, not yet acted on.** The owner was recommended to buy (1) _Der junge Koch / Die junge Köchin_, (2) Harold McGee, _On Food and Cooking_, (3) J. Kenji López-Alt, _The Food Lab_; later Pauli, _Lehrbuch der Küche_, and Jeffrey Hamelman, _Bread_. The owner has bought none of them yet, so no note rests on any of them today.
- **A citation needs someone to look up the page in the book.** A note names a book only when a person has read the passage in the book itself; an agent cannot read these books, and nothing is cited from memory. The place (chapter, section, page) is part of the source entry ([data model](data-model.md#technique)).
- **Two new evidence levels** next to `studied`, `textbook` and `tradition`: `tested` (shown as "Küchenversuch" / "Kitchen test"; the author ran an experiment and reports it, for example a tested-kitchen book) and `authority` (shown as "Behörde" / "Authority"; an official body states it, for food safety). No shipped note was re-labelled; all stay `textbook`. See [data model](data-model.md#technique) and [classic format](classic-format.md).
- **Food-safety figures are cited from the authority of the user's language**, not from one source for everyone: the BfR for German, the USDA FSIS or the UK Food Standards Agency for English. The numbers differ between authorities (finding 3 below), so the note says whose figure it is. The data holds no such figure today.
- **The licence of the sources** is [decided apart](decisions/sources-licence.md): non-commercial use is fine for this hobby project.

## How to read this file

- **Verification marks.** `V-cat` = title, author, year, publisher or ISBN seen in a library or bookseller catalogue record returned by the Google Books catalogue feed or a web search on 2026-11-19. It is a catalogue record, not a publisher page, so "edition number", "in print" and "price" are usually **not verified**. `V-page` = seen on the publisher's or an official page. `NV` = not verified. Nothing was guessed; where a fact is missing it says `NV`.
- **Judgement** = my assessment from what is well established about the book, not something looked up. It is marked as such.
- **Evidence level** (maps to the app's note levels, see section 5): (a) peer-reviewed science or science textbook, (b) professional-training textbook, (c) tested-kitchen work, (d) authoritative tradition or reference work, (e) popular book.
- No page numbers or quotations from the books are given here; the project rule is that a locator is read in the book itself, in the session that writes the note.

## 1. Summary and recommended core set

### What the repository has today (counted from `packages/engine/data`)

- 64 shipped notes (techniques, handling rules, substitutes), **all level `textbook`**, 149 source entries. Every one cites a vocational textbook on workforce.libretexts.org or BCcampus: Culinary Foundations, Flavors of the Saucier, Fruits/Vegetables/Farinaceous Products, Introduction to Baking and Pastries, Understanding Ingredients for the Canadian Baker, Food Preparation Lab Manual, Garde Manger, Meat Cutting. Licences: 128 CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, 16 CC BY 4.0, 5 CC BY-NC 4.0.
- Templates cite Ruhlman, _Ratio_ (checked in `docs/ratios.md`).
- So: no peer-reviewed or food-science source, no German-language source, no tested-kitchen source, and mostly one source per claim. The levels `studied` and `tradition` exist in the app but no shipped note uses them.

**Food safety (not books, the authority for temperature skills):** USDA FSIS safe minimum internal temperature chart; FDA Food Code (2026 edition issued); UK Food Standards Agency cooking guidance; German BfR consumer leaflets; NCHFP (already used). They _disagree in numbers_ (see finding 3), which is itself teachable.

**Free or open in the set:** Baldwin's sous vide review paper (open access, CC BY-NC-ND 3.0 per Crossref) and his free web guide for sous vide skills; Escoffier in the public-domain 1903/1907/1909 editions for sauces; Fannie Farmer 1896/1918 and Mrs Beeton 1861 for old method wording; the existing LibreTexts books stay as second source (with the licence question below).

Why these: (1) every skill area gets at least one _explaining_ source (McGee, Belitz) and one _practice_ source (CIA, Pauli, Junge Koch); (2) two independent German professional textbooks plus a German chemistry text satisfy "German users matter"; (3) the tested-kitchen book gives the app an honest "someone ran the experiment" level; (4) the set is recognisable, i.e. a user can look it up in a library.

### Three most important findings

1. **Today's whole evidence base is one family of open vocational textbooks under CC BY-NC-SA.** That is good for transparency, but (a) nothing is science-grade, (b) nothing is German, (c) the NC and SA terms deserve a legal read (open question 1). A second, independent authority per skill is the main gap.
2. **Access is the bottleneck, not choice.** The project rule is "every statement from a source read in the session". Of the core set only the following are readable by an agent without the owner: public-domain old books (archive.org), the Baldwin paper, official food-safety pages, and Google Books previews of some titles. The German professional textbooks (Pauli, Der junge Koch) have no free preview that I could reach, and their publisher hosts are blocked here. The owner has to buy or lend them and hand over passages (scans or photographs of the page), or the host list must be widened.
3. **Authorities disagree on food-safety numbers, and a single cited number would mislead.** From search results (the FSIS and BfR pages themselves were not reachable): FSIS gives 145 °F (63 °C) plus a 3 minute rest for whole cuts of beef, pork, veal and lamb, 160 °F (71 °C) for ground meat, 165 °F (74 °C) for poultry; the UK FSA baseline is 70 °C for 2 minutes with equivalent time-temperature pairs (for example 60 °C for 45 minutes, 75 °C for 30 seconds); the BfR says 70 °C at the core, held for 2 minutes. The skill "judging doneness / food safety temperatures" should cite the authority of the user's locale and say so.

## 5. Proposal for the app's citation data

### Why a separate source record

Today every note repeats `title`, `place`, `url`, `licence` and a `sentence` inline (see `sear.json`). The same book appears many times with slightly different titles (for example "Flavors of the Saucier: Stocks, Sauces and Soups" vs "Stocks, Sauces, and Soups" appears twice in the data). A source table (one record per work) plus a short locator per use fixes this, keeps edition information in one place and lets the UI show one short line.

### Source record (one per work and edition, in `packages/engine/data/sources.json`)

| Field                | Example                                                    | Note                                        |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| `id`                 | `mcgee-2004`                                               | stable key                                  |
| `kind`               | `book`, `paper`, `web`, `official`, `open-textbook`        |                                             |
| `authors`            | `["Harold McGee"]`                                         | short form for display: `McGee`             |
| `shortTitle`         | `{ en: "On Food and Cooking", de: "On Food and Cooking" }` | display                                     |
| `title`              | full title and subtitle                                    |                                             |
| `edition`            | `2004 revised edition`                                     | the label the book itself uses              |
| `year`               | `2004`                                                     |                                             |
| `publisher`, `place` | `Scribner`                                                 |                                             |
| `language`           | `en`                                                       | so the UI can say "englisch"                |
| `isbn`               | `9780684800011`                                            | only if verified; `isbnChecked: "catalogue" | "publisher" | "book in hand"` |
| `doi`                | for papers                                                 |                                             |
| `evidence`           | `a`, `b`, `c`, `d`, `e`                                    | see mapping below                           |
| `url`                | link to a free legal version, else the catalogue record    |                                             |
| `licence`            | `CC BY-NC-SA 4.0`, `public domain`, `none (cited as fact)` | what the app may reuse                      |
| `access`             | `free`, `library`, `paid`, `borrow`                        | shown on "where to read it"                 |
| `verified`           | `{ by, at, how }`                                          | who saw the edition data and how            |
| `notes`              | known errors, disputes, edition differences                | not shown by default                        |

### Locator (on the note, one per citation)

| Field                | Example                                                                                           |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `source`             | `mcgee-2004`                                                                                      |
| `locator`            | `{ chapter: "3", page: "152" }` or `{ section: "6.2" }` or `{ position: "PT94", kind: "e-book" }` |
| `sentence`           | the returned sentence (at most 25 words), as already required by the helper plan                  |
| `retrieved`          | date for web sources                                                                              |
| `agrees` / `differs` | optional: marks that another authority gives another number                                       |

A note then has `sources: [ { source, locator, sentence } ]` and its level is computed or set (see below). Locators of e-books must be marked as `kind: "e-book"`, since positions differ from printed pages.

### Short line shown to the user

`{authors short} {shortTitle}, {year}, {locator}` plus a label for the level, for example:

- `McGee, On Food and Cooking, 2004, S. 152 · Lehrbuch`
- `Baldwin, Sous vide cooking: a review, 2012, S. 15-30 · Untersucht`

A tap opens the long form: full title, edition, publisher, language, ISBN, where to read it (free link or library), and the returned sentence.

### Mapping evidence levels to what the user sees

The app has three labels today (`strings-classics.ts`): **Untersucht** (a peer-reviewed study measured it), **Lehrbuch** (a cooking or food-science textbook states it), **Überlieferung** (kitchens do it; no test found).

| Evidence                                                        | Shown as                                                                             | Rule                                                                                    |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| (a) peer-reviewed paper                                         | Untersucht / Studied                                                                 | Only when the _paper itself_ was read for this claim                                    |
| (a) science textbook (Belitz, McGee when it states a mechanism) | Lehrbuch / Textbook                                                                  | A textbook is a textbook; mention "Food-Science" in the long form                       |
| (b) professional-training textbook                              | Lehrbuch / Textbook                                                                  |                                                                                         |
| (c) tested-kitchen work                                         | **new: "Küchenversuch" / "Kitchen-tested"**, or fold into Lehrbuch (owner to decide) | The experiment is reported but not peer reviewed; I would not call it "studied"         |
| (d) tradition or reference work                                 | Überlieferung / Tradition                                                            | The classics, Escoffier, Larousse, Ruhlman's ratios                                     |
| (e) popular book                                                | Überlieferung with "Einschätzung der Autorin" in the long form                       | Or the project's "judgement" mark. A popular book alone never lifts a claim to Lehrbuch |
| Official (FSIS, BfR, FSA, FDA)                                  | **new: "Behörde" / "Official"**                                                      | Distinct because it is the authority for a number, and regional                         |

A note's level should be the _highest level that two sources, or one source, support as read_, and a note with only one source of level d or e should say so ("eine Quelle"). Where authorities disagree, show both and the user's locale first.

### Rights field in practice

Cite facts, write explanations in our own words, quote at most the 25 words the plan already allows, never take a table or a list wholesale. For CC BY-NC-SA open books keep the attribution line in the long form (the data already stores the licence).

## 6. Rights notes

- **Normal citation is enough** for every book in the core set: a fact, a number or a mechanism is cited with author, title, edition, page; the explanation is written in our own words (project rule). Nothing needs a permission.
- **Do not extract tables or lists as data sets:** Belitz (property tables), Gisslen and CIA (yield and conversion tables), McGee (tables of temperatures), Flavor Bible and Flavour Thesaurus (pairing lists), Modernist Cuisine (time-temperature tables). The compilation is the publisher's; in the EU a database right may apply as well (judgement, not a legal opinion). A single number cited with its page is a different matter from a table copied into `data/`.
- **Pairing books:** the pairings come from chef interviews (Flavor Bible) and one author's judgement (Segnit); individual pairings as facts are citeable, but a bulk import of the lists would take a substantial part of the book and would also sit badly with the project's ban on FlavorDB. Judgement: use them only per cited pairing, marked "judgement", never as a data set.
- **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 books (the current core of the data):** the NC term forbids commercial use of the material, and SA requires that an adaptation is released under the same licence. The repository ships short sentences and own-word notes. Whether an own-word note based on a CC BY-NC-SA text is an "adaptation" is a legal question I cannot settle here. If the app stays non-commercial and its data are published under compatible terms, it is probably fine (judgement); the owner should decide, and ask whether the data licence of the repository itself should be CC BY-NC-SA (open question 1). The BCcampus books in CC BY 4.0 have no such problem.
- **Public domain:** Escoffier 1903/1907 (EN 1907/1909), Fannie Farmer 1896/1918, Mrs Beeton, Davidis and Prato. In the US anything published before 1931 is in the public domain; in the EU the author must have died more than 70 years ago (Escoffier d. 1935, Beeton d. 1865, Farmer d. 1915, Davidis d. 1876: judgement, dates from memory, NV). Modern translations and reprints are separately copyrighted (Cracknell/Kaufmann translation of Escoffier, 1979 and 2011; facsimile editions of Farmer carry their own front matter).
- **Government material:** US federal websites (FSIS, FDA) are public domain as federal works (judgement; the pages' own statement was not read because FSIS answered 403). UK government pages are normally under the Open Government Licence (NV). EU documents are normally reusable with attribution (NV). The BfR's terms of reuse were not read (NV): citing its numbers with a link is certainly fine, copying its leaflets is not covered.
- **Baldwin paper:** open access, CC BY-NC-ND 3.0 (from the Crossref licence record). ND means no derivatives of the paper itself; citing and explaining in own words does not make a derivative of the paper (judgement).
