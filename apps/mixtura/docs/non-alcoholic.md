# What ethanol does and what replaces it

The practice document names five jobs[^bainbridge]. Result of checking:

| Job of ethanol            | Claim                                                                                                                                                                             | Evidence                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Solvent and aroma release | Ethanol changes how volatile aroma compounds partition and move out of the liquid; in a 12 % ethanol solution, mass transfer of 17 compounds was higher than in water[^tsachaki]. | `read` (abstract, one model system at 12 % ethanol; no statement for cocktails). That ethanol also extracts non-water-soluble flavour from botanicals is common knowledge: `assumption, unverified`. |
| Body, viscosity           | Ethanol-water mixtures are more viscous than either pure liquid at some ratios.                                                                                                   | `assumption, unverified`: searches reached no physical-chemistry source; no value is given.                                                                                                          |
| Bite (heat, "burn")       | Replaced by ginger, chilli, tannin from tea, acid, carbonation.                                                                                                                   | Practice only (practice document); no measurement seen.                                                                                                                                              |
| Preservation              | Ethanol suppresses microbes at higher concentrations.                                                                                                                             | `assumption, unverified` for any concentration. Consequence kept: a drink without ethanol gets no preservation credit; see [shelf life](#shelf-life-and-food-safety).                                |
| Dilution behaviour        | The balance model is `V = V0 (1 + f)` with `f` by method ([balance](balance.md#dilution-model)).                                                                                  | See below.                                                                                                                                                                                           |

**Dilution without ethanol.** The model of [balance](balance.md) already
takes water of dilution as a fraction of the volume before dilution
and its fractions are all `secondary` or `assumption`. Arnold's point
that dilution depends on the drink's components and ABV, and that drinks at
the same final temperature have the same dilution[^arnold], was seen only
as a summary. A drink without ethanol and with sugar freezes at a
different temperature, so the dilution fraction of a shaken
non-alcoholic drink may differ. **No source was found for the size of
that difference.** Proposed: the engine keeps the existing fractions for
drinks without alcohol and marks the result `assumption, unverified`
(reuse of the balance evidence status), until a measurement is read.
That keeps the shown ABV after dilution correct in any case, because
it is computed from the volume.

What replaces each job is practice, not measurement. The classes
below say what each carries; none of the following is a source for the
claim that it "replaces" ethanol.

# Ingredient classes

Properties are those of the [data model](data-model.md#ingredient)
(`role`, `abv`, `sugar`, `acid`, `shelfLifeDays`). A value without a
source is `assumption, unverified` and must stay editable data.

| Class                                | Examples                                             | What it contributes in a drink                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Non-alcoholic distillate or aperitif | Seedlip, Lyre's, non-alcoholic bitter aperitifs      | depth, a base for a sour or a highball; strengths differ by product and are read from the label |
| Tea and tisane                       | black tea, rooibos, hibiscus, smoked tea             | tannin (bite), aroma, base                                                                      |
| Verjus                               | white or red verjus                                  | acid with some fruit character; milder than citrus                                              |
| Shrub, vinegar                       | fruit and vinegar shrub, drinking vinegar            | acid and sugar in one prepared ingredient                                                       |
| Ferment                              | kombucha, water kefir, tepache                       | acid, carbonation, depth; contains some ethanol                                                 |
| Acid solution                        | citric, malic, tartaric acid in water ("acid blend") | acid without flavour; strength is the maker's choice                                            |
| Saline                               | salt solution in dropper                             | lifts flavour; used in drops                                                                    |
| Bitters, alcohol-based               | Angostura, Peychaud's                                | depth in drops; contains ethanol                                                                |
| Bitters, non-alcoholic               | glycerin- or water-based bitters                     | depth in drops without ethanol                                                                  |
| Sweetener                            | simple syrup, rich syrup, honey, agave               | sugar (balance)                                                                                 |
| Texture agent                        | glycerin, gum syrup, aquafaba                        | body without sugar or with it                                                                   |
| Juice                                | lemon, lime, apple                                   | acid, sugar, fruit                                                                              |

Claims and evidence per class:

- **Ferments contain ethanol.** Kombucha from retail and producers in
  British Columbia: 684 samples, mean 0.77 %, median 0.62 %, range
  not detected to 3.62 % ABV, 31.5 % of samples above the province's 1 %
  limit for non-alcoholic beverages; naturally fermented kombucha is
  stated to contain 0 to 3 % ABV[^jang] (`read`, abstract). Brazilian
  kombucha is classed "low alcohol" below 0.5 % and "alcoholic" from 0.5
  to 8 % [^okaru] (`secondary`: quoted in the paper). Water kefir: the
  ethanol content "may be present at varying levels"[^sy] (`read`; no
  value). Tepache after 72 h of fermentation: 9.5 Brix and acidic pH,
  ethanol and acetic acid among the volatile compounds[^tepache]
  (`read`, abstract; ethanol value not read). So no typical ABV can be
  given for a ferment; the data holds the value the user measured or
  the label states, and an unmeasured ferment has `abv` unknown, not 0.
- **Kombucha pH.** Samples generally between 2.5 and 3.8[^njieukam]
  (`read`, quoted from the paper's discussion of the literature).
- **Home-brewed kombucha under a controlled recipe.** NCHFP's recipe
  (black and green tea, 1 cup sugar to 1 gallon, SCOBY with starter
  liquid, 7 to 10 days at 68 to 77 °F) requires a starter liquid of pH
  4.0 or less and says that kombucha made by it "may have trace amounts
  of alcohol (<0.016 % alcohol by volume - ABV)"[^nchfp-kombucha][^nchfp-kombucha-faq]
  (`read`). This does not contradict the British Columbia range: those
  were retail and producer samples without that recipe's controls[^jang].
  A recipe value is not a property of "kombucha": the data holds the
  value for the user's own batch.
- **Alcohol-based bitters.** Angostura 44.7 % and Peychaud's 35 %
  ABV, one dash about 0.92 ml (1/32 oz), other estimates 0.7 to 0.8 ml[^bitters]
  (`secondary`: consumer pages; no manufacturer page read; these pages
  were not among the hosts opened on 2026-11-17). Ethanol per
  dash `derived`: 0.92 ml × 0.447 = 0.41 ml for Angostura, 0.92 ml ×
  0.35 = 0.32 ml for Peychaud's. Effect on a drink, `derived`: two dashes
  of Angostura (1.84 ml, 0.82 ml ethanol) in a finished drink of about
  120 ml come to about 0.7 % ABV; one dash in 150 ml about 0.3 %. So a
  drink with two dashes of alcohol-based bitters is above 0.5 % by
  calculation. The inputs are `secondary`; the point of the
  calculation is that the app must compute, not assume 0.
- **Non-alcoholic distillates and aperitifs.** Strength is on each
  label. This research found no label or data sheet (hosts blocked) and
  gives no ABV per product: `assumption, unverified`. UK and EU terms
  for a bottle labelled "alcohol-free" are in [the legal section](#legal-terms).
- **Acid.** A reference acid and the content of a juice are in
  [ingredients](ingredients.md) and [balance](balance.md); this research
  did not verify new juice or verjus values (`assumption, unverified`).
  The searches for verjus and citrus acidity returned no usable source.
- **Glycerin, gum syrup, aquafaba.** Searches gave no source for the
  sweetness or viscosity of glycerin and none for the shelf life of
  aquafaba (`assumption, unverified`). Studies on aquafaba's foaming
  exist (for example PMID 41895942, 2026) but their abstracts were not
  read for this document.

# How the six templates carry over

Roles are those of [templates](templates.md#ratios). Without a spirit
the `base` slot is filled by a different class; the slot and ratio
stay, so the match and the range check keep working. Whether the
usual ranges of [templates](templates.md#evidence) (all assumptions)
fit a drink without ethanol is **open**: the ABV range is meaningless
for a non-alcoholic drink, the sugar and acid ranges are
`assumption`.

| Template      | Role that changes                                                                               | Non-alcoholic form (practice, no source)                                         |
| ------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Old-Fashioned | `base`: tea, non-alcoholic distillate; `bitter`: drops, ethanol-free bitters                    | stirred, small sugar part                                                        |
| Martini       | `base`: non-alcoholic distillate; `modifier`: verjus or a tea; `bitter`: non-alcoholic aperitif | the modifier carries acid and the bitter slot is optional                        |
| Daiquiri      | `base`: tea or distillate; `sour`: citrus, verjus, acid solution; `sweet`: syrup                | the sour template carries over unchanged                                         |
| Sidecar       | `modifier`: a non-alcoholic fruit liqueur substitute or a shrub                                 | the same as the Daiquiri with a flavoured third part                             |
| Highball      | `base`: distillate, tea, ferment; `lengthener`: soda, tonic, ginger beer                        | carries over; ferments as lengthener add ethanol, see the table above            |
| Flip          | `base`: tea or distillate; `texture`: aquafaba instead of egg                                   | the texture slot is why aquafaba is listed; shelf life of aquafaba is unverified |

# Shelf life and food safety

**This document gives no shelf life as a fact for a bar-made
ingredient.** The agencies read on 2026-11-17 define limits by pH,
water activity, temperature and process. The storage times they print
belong to specific, controlled recipes. Each is quoted below with its
conditions and does not transfer to another recipe. All statements in
this section are `read` unless marked otherwise.

**Limits the agencies state.**

- **21 CFR 114.3** (eCFR, text of 2026-11-10)[^cfr114]: acid foods have a
  natural pH of 4.6 or below (a); acidified foods are low-acid foods to
  which acid or acid food is added, with water activity above 0.85 and a
  finished equilibrium pH of 4.6 or below (b); low-acid foods are any
  foods other than alcoholic beverages with a finished equilibrium pH
  above 4.6 and water activity above 0.85 (d). Paragraph (b) excludes
  from the part carbonated beverages, jams, jellies, preserves and "foods
  that are stored, distributed, and retailed under refrigeration".
  These are definitions for commercial canned and bottled foods, not
  storage instructions. The 4.6 line is the regulation's line for
  botulism control; it is no storage time for a syrup, and refrigerated
  foods are outside the part.
- **FDA, refrigerated low-acid juices** (guidance of June 2007, page
  current as of 2018-09-20)[^fda-carrot]: carrot and other low-acid juices
  "can pose a risk of botulism poisoning" if not processed against
  _C. botulinum_ spores and then kept without proper refrigeration; four US
  and two Canadian cases occurred in 2006. FDA recommends validated
  controls such as "acidification of the juice to a pH of 4.6 or below"
  or a thermal treatment, plus a "Keep Refrigerated" label statement. The
  guidance is for juice processors under 21 CFR 120 and says it does not
  pertain to other foods that need refrigeration by consumers.
- **BfR, botulism FAQ** (published 2025-12-11)[^bfr-botulism]:
  _C. botulinum_ multiplies poorly in acid foods, so acid foods carry a
  low botulism risk. BfR collected no pH data itself and points to the
  Clemson University lists, by which most fruit and berries have a pH
  below 4.6, with exceptions such as melons, ripe mango and possibly
  pears; these figures vary with variety, ripeness and water content and
  offer no certainty (BfR's words, translated). Low-acid food (pH above 4.6) should be canned
  under pressure at 121 °C or first acidified, for example with vinegar
  or lemon juice, until the measured pH is below 4.6.
- **FSA, botulism** (published 25 June 2026)[^fsa-botulism]: home-preserved
  foods, foods preserved in oil and food packed in airtight containers
  have been associated with foodborne botulism; canning and bottling
  herbs or garlic in oil is risky at home "as it is difficult to achieve
  the level of control needed". The page gives no pH or storage time.
- **Refrigerator temperature.** FDA: 40 °F (4.4 °C) or below[^fda-fridge];
  FSA: between 0 and 5 °C[^fsa-chill]; BfR: at most 7 °C, better below 5 °C[^bfr-cooling].
  BfR adds that there is no general home storage temperature for all
  foods and that each food needs its own decision[^bfr-cooling].

**Fresh juice.** In the US a juice that has not been processed to a 5-log
reduction of the pertinent microorganism must carry the label "WARNING:
This product has not been pasteurized and, therefore, may contain harmful
bacteria that can cause serious illness in children, the elderly, and
persons with weakened immune systems" (21 CFR 101.17(g)(2) and (7))[^juice].
Juice HACCP does not apply to a retail establishment, defined as an
operation that provides juice directly to consumers (21 CFR 120.3: the
definition of processing excludes "the operation of a retail
establishment")[^juice]. **Correction of the first pass:** it said retail
sellers remain bound by the labelling rule. The text of 101.17(g) has no
retail exemption, but whether the warning must be on juice served
unpackaged at a bar is not settled by the text read; that stays open.

**UK consultation of 2023.** The consultation page of 28 September 2023
says it ran to 23 November 2023 and still reads "We are analysing your

for a government response found none)[^uk-consult]. Its subject: raising
the "alcohol free" line from 0.05 % to 0.5 % ABV, which the government news
story calls "in line with" the USA, Denmark, Germany, Australia, Sweden,
Portugal and Belgium[^uk-news], and whether to drop "de-alcoholised". So the
UK figures above are the 2018 guidance as quoted in 2023; **no
confirmation that they are unchanged in 2026 was found beyond that the
consultation page shows no outcome.** Status: `read` for the proposal,
outcome unknown.

**Corrections of the first pass.** (1) UK: "non-alcoholic" is not a
term reserved for sacramental wine; guidance only forbids it next to a
name commonly associated with an alcoholic drink, with an exception for
communion wine, and sets no strength line for it. (2) EU wine: the line is
"no more than 0.5 %" for de-alcoholised wine, not "below". (3) US: malt
beverages have a third line, "low alcohol" below 2.5 %, and "0.0 percent"
is allowed only for a drink labelled "alcohol free" that holds no alcohol.
(4) The 1.2 % figure is the UK "low alcohol" line; in the EU it is where
the strength-labelling and claims rules begin, not a "low alcohol" term.
(5) Germany: for wine the rule is now read: "alkoholfrei" only beside the
EU term "de-alcoholised"; the brewers' 0.5 % line for beer is still
`secondary`.

Other countries in the review (`secondary`): Spain 1 % vol and France
1.2 % vol for alcohol-free beer, Japan 1 %, China 0.5 %[^okaru].

**What the app may claim.** The app computes ABV from ingredient data
that the user or the seed data entered; it measures nothing. Laws and
guidance above apply to a labelled product and to measured strength. The
app therefore does not state that a drink is "alcohol-free",
"non-alcoholic" or "de-alcoholised" in the legal sense, and does not say
a drink is safe to drink. It shows the computed ABV, says it is
calculated, and says which inputs are unknown. This is a proposal for the
owner (`needs decision`).

# Proposed templates and variants

Justified by the claims above, not by a source that says they work:

1. **Non-alcoholic sour** (variant of Daiquiri): `base` tea or
   non-alcoholic distillate, `sour` citrus, verjus or acid solution,
   `sweet` syrup. Shake.
2. **Non-alcoholic highball** (variant of Highball): `base` distillate or
   tea, `lengthener` soda, tonic or a ferment. Ferment as lengthener
   raises ABV: kombucha 0 to 3.62 % observed[^jang].

   aperitif plus a lengthener; the existing templates have no `bitter`
   slot in the highball, so a new optional slot is needed.
4. **Shrub drink**: `sour` and `sweet` from one prepared shrub plus a
   `lengthener`.
5. **Foamed drink** (variant of Flip): aquafaba in the `texture` slot.
6. **Low-ABV stirred drink** (variant of Martini): `base` fortified or
   aromatised wine at its label ABV, optional `bitter`; computed ABV
   shown.

Whether these are variants of existing templates or new templates is

## Shelf life with entered dates

The document above gives no shelf life as a fact and the app never proposes
one: a prepared ingredient has an optional **shelf life in days, the user's own
value** (`shelfLifeDays`) and an optional **storage text in the user's words**
(`storage`). The date arithmetic is the engine's (`expiry`,
`limitingShelfLife`, [engine](engine.md#shelf)); the engine has no clock, so
the app passes today's date from the device (`src/today.ts`).

- **Where the batch date lives.** In the stock record of the prepared
  ingredient: `openedOn` is the day the current batch was made and `present`
  says it is at hand ([data model](data-model.md#stock)). No new store, no
  migration, no format bump: the field is in the schema and in the export
  already, and the use-by date is derived every time (made on plus the shelf
  life) and never stored. The engine's makeable does not read the date: a
  batch past its date is still "in stock" for the stock rules; the app says
  so in words. "I made this" on the spec screen
  replaces the date of the newest batch. Several batches with their own dates
  are kept from #101 on: the further dates are `moreBatches` on the same
  stock record, shown and changed on the Stock screen
  ([as built](screens-stock-settings.md#batches-near-their-date-and-more-than-one-batch-101)).
- **What the app says** (batch card on the spec screen of the prepared
  ingredient's recipe): "Made on 2026-11-17 · use by 2026-11-27", then one of
  "Within its shelf life, 13 days left", "Use soon: 2 days left" (the use-by
  date is within `USE_SOON_DAYS` = 2 days, today included; the window is an
  assumption, unverified, no source gives one, and a parameter of `expiry`),
  or "Past the date you set by 8 days. Check it before you use it." A check
  or a dash goes with the words; nothing is red or an alarm; no verdict about
  the drink or the user. Without a shelf life: "No shelf life entered".
- **Nested preparation.** The shortest of the user's own values on the
  prepared ingredient and on the prepared ingredients inside it counts, and
  the card names the ingredient that sets it ("Shelf life: 14 days, set by
  Syrup"). Raw parts carry no shelf life in the data and say nothing.
- **Vectors** `packages/engine/vectors/shelf/`: every state, the boundary
  days (3, 2, 0 days left, the day after), a window of 0 and of 5 days,
  shelf life 0, calendar edges (month end, leap day, year end), unknown
  (no shelf life, a date that is not real, a fraction of a day), nested
  limiting (own longer, own none, a tie, a cycle).

## Variant suggestions

On a drink that follows a template with a variant, the spec screen offers
"Start a Riff as Non-Alcoholic Sour" (or the low-ABV variant). It opens the
editor as a riff on the drink with the variant as its template, the lines of
role `base` left out, every other line carried over unchanged and an empty
base line to fill; the drink's method and glass stay. The card states what is
replaced and carried over and that the taste and the balance change; it never
claims the result tastes the same. The variants are the four of
[As built (#36)](#as-built-36), so their slots and ratios are assumptions and
the card says so. A variant of the class the drink already has is not
offered; Old-Fashioned and Sidecar have none and show no card. Vectors
`packages/engine/vectors/variants/` (sour, highball, stirred, flip, two base
lines, no variant, already a variant, no template).

**Deviations from the issue.** The suggestion opens the editor (a riff), not a
swap session, because a swap only exchanges single lines and the variant
removes the base and adds a slot; no balance figures are shown for a
suggestion, because without a chosen base there is no figure and the nominal
examples are labelled as such; the batch is a field of the stock record, not
a `batches` store; the list of batches near their date on the Stock screen
is not built (follow-up issue).
